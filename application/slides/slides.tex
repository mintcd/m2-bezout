\begin{frame}{Research Experience: Analysis of Algorithms}
  Example of a regular expression (regex): $(a+b\cdot c)\cdot d\cdot e^*$, equivalent to $(a \cdot d+b \cdot c \cdot d)e^*$.

  Hyperscan executes as follows
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item extracts literals ($a\cdot d$ and $b\cdot c\cdot d$ in the example) from a regex,
    \item matches all literals using FDR or another string matcher\footnote{Qiu et al. (2021). Teddy: An efficient SIMD-based literal matching engine for scalable deep packet inspection.} \textit{at once},
    \item verifies subregex matches using the previous results.
  \end{enumerate}

  We assume a distribution over the regex trees, currently the BST-like family\footnote{Binary Search Tree}.

  \only<1-7>{A useful statistics is the length of longest literals in average, which leads to analyzing the length $Y_n$ of longest literals  that an $n$-node subtree \textit{yields} to its parent.
  $$Y_{n+2} = \underbrace{I^{(n)}}_{\substack{\text{indicator of root} \\ \text{being concat.}}}\underbrace{\left(Y_{U_n} + Y_{n+1-U_n} + 1\right)}_{\substack{\text{take all of the subtrees,}\\ \text{plus the current}}} + \underbrace{J^{(n)}}_{\substack{\text{indicator of root} \\ \text{being alternation}}} \underbrace{\max\left(Y_{U_n}, Y_{n+1-U_n}\right)}_{\substack{\text{take the longer}\\\text{of the subtrees}}}, \quad Y_1=Y_2=0.$$
  }

\begin{frame}{PhD Topic}
  \textbf{Context and Motivation:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Developers optimize their library algorithms in a fine-grained way e.g., they make assumptions on the input or leverage hardware features such as SIMD instructions.
    \item Researchers are interested in explaining if such optimizations are indeed efficient. Examples include both confirmation (TimSort\footnote{Auger, N., Jugé, V., Nicaud, C., \& Pivoteau, C. (2018). On the worst-case complexity of TimSort.}) and rejection (Lua's hybrid table\footnote{Martínez, C., Nicaud, C., \& Rotondo, P. (2022). A probabilistic model revealing shortcomings in Lua’s hybrid tables.})
  \end{itemize}

  \textbf{Goal:} Develop a framework for analyzing algorithms that use SIMD.

  \textbf{Fruitfulness:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Direct applications to algorithm design.
    \item Requirements naturally lead to deep mathematical questions, e.g., $\mathbb{E}[Y_n]$ may not be analyzable by classical techniques.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
